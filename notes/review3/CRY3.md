# Review 3: CRY3 (cryptography and races)

Delta 9e7711f..HEAD. Checked and found sound: chunk AEAD layout (index, count and final flag in AAD; per-file key; separate thumbnail key; cross-file swap fails because keys differ), root-block/CID binding, envelope `id` over `signed` bytes (channelToken inside `signed` stops cross-token reuse; powProof stripping cannot change `id`), threshold multi-signer rules (distinct DIDs, candidate set), `op`/`ctx` separation of admission-key signatures (envelope, pin/seed, upload, sequence, SFU), registration replay (per-token monotonic `ts`), SFrame KID with blank leaves, broadcast `device` field.

### CRY3-1: Stamped-Commit "counts" rule forks catch-up members; the 10 s check uses a sender-chosen timestamp
- Severity: P1
- Location: spec/05-messaging.md:31, spec/05-messaging.md:34 (the paragraph after the two criteria)
- Evidence: "**Published in time.** The `ts` of the routing envelope carrying it (§5.2.2) is no more than 10 seconds after the `ts` of its stamp"; "a member keeps its epoch N state until 15 seconds after the stamp `ts` of the Commit it applied for epoch N, and switches to a counting Commit at an earlier position that arrives in that time; it discards Commits for epoch N that arrive later." §5.2.2: "`ts` is the sender's clock". §11.4: an envelope is `expired` only if its `ts` is more than 300 s ahead or "older than the `retentionDays`".
- Problem: Two inputs to the winner decision are not shared by all members. (1) The envelope `ts` is set by the publisher, so criterion 1 is satisfied by any envelope whose `ts` is set to the stamp `ts`, however late it is actually published; Relays admit it for the whole retention period. (2) "Arrives in that time" is local arrival time. Attack: a committer gets Commit A stamped at position n, withholds it, lets B (position n+1) win at online members, then publishes A 30 s later with `ts` = stamp `ts`. Online members discard A (past their hold). Every member that was offline and catches up from relay history (§5.2.5) receives A and B together, finds both "count", and applies A (lower position). Those members are on a different epoch N+1 and cannot process later traffic. Any member allowed to commit can do this at will; an honest committer whose envelope is delayed more than about 5 s in relay propagation causes the same split. This is the split the sequencer exists to prevent ("A Space avoids this by designating a sequencer").
- Suggested fix: Make the winner a function of the sequencer log only. The log already holds each `op`, which is the exact `sealedEnvelope` bytes. Rule: the winning Commit for epoch N is the first position on the commit stream whose `op` is a valid Commit for epoch N. A member that receives a stamped Commit for epoch N at position p fetches the commit stream log up to p (§14.4.3) before applying, and applies the first valid Commit for epoch N it finds there, whether or not that Commit was ever published. Drop the publication criterion and the 15 s hold. If the log is unreachable the member waits, as committers already do.
- Needs decision: yes. (a) Winner chosen from the log alone, as above (recommended: deterministic, nothing to stall on, no clocks). (b) Keep the windows but have Relays parse `commitStamp` and refuse an envelope whose stamp `ts` is more than 10 s before the Relay's clock. This makes criterion 1 enforceable for history, but online members still diverge on arrival within the 15 s hold. (c) Keep the text and state that catch-up members can fork (this contradicts the purpose of the sequencer).

### CRY3-2: "Valid" criterion depends on each member's current Space state
- Severity: P2
- Location: spec/05-messaging.md:32
- Evidence: "made by a sender authorized for every change it makes under current Space state (§4)"; "Every member skips a stamped Commit that does not count".
- Problem: Members hold different Space state heads while a new state is propagating (an admin revoked, a `commitPolicy` changed). Members on the old head count a Commit that members on the new head skip, so they apply different Commits for the same epoch, and the group forks. Whether a Commit counts must depend only on data every member holds identically.
- Suggested fix: Base counting on MLS validity only (open, decrypt, RFC 9420 §12.4.2, §5.2.1 checks). Treat an authorization failure under Space state as a policy violation (§4.5) handled by a later Commit, never as "does not count". Alternative: bind the check to a Space state CID carried in the stamped submission. That adds a field and still needs every member to have fetched that CID.
- Needs decision: no

### CRY3-3: Commits on the new token only, so members that have not refetched the key miss them for up to the grace period
- Severity: P2
- Location: spec/05-messaging.md:27, spec/14-jmap.md:170, spec/05-messaging.md:267
- Evidence: "Commits are stamped only on the current token's commit stream: once the CO registers a rotated key ... the sequencer stamps no further Commit on the old token's commit stream". Member duties: fetch the key "on reconnect, after processing a Commit that removes a member, when a relay reports that a `channelToken` is no longer admitted (§11), and at least once an hour"; the old token is withdrawn after a grace "RECOMMENDED to be 300 seconds".
- Problem: After a rotation that the member has not caused (periodic, compromise, a removal it has not yet processed), the first Commit is published under the new `channelToken`, which a member still on the old key neither knows nor follows. That member misses the Commit until `token-withdrawn` arrives (300 s or more), or for up to an hour. Meanwhile it keeps sending application messages in epoch N on the old token. Other members have moved to N+1 and keep the previous sealing key for only 60 s (Appendix limits table), so those messages are lost. The member also misses the 15 s hold window of §5.1.1 and becomes a catch-up member, which exposes it to CRY3-1. Before this delta, Commits could still be stamped on the old token during the grace period, so this is a regression.
- Suggested fix: When a Relay applies a registration whose key has `replaces`, it sends each connection subscribed to the replaced token an `Error` without `id` carrying that token, with a new code `token-rotated` (or reuses `token-withdrawn` with the subscription kept open until deregistration). On that code the client fetches the current `ChannelAdmissionKey`. Relays already receive `replaces` and currently ignore it (§10.5.3), so this adds no new linkage.
- Needs decision: no

### CRY3-4: DMMembers has no read path, no defined `seq`, and no ordering against MLS Commits, so the host list and the group diverge
- Severity: P2
- Location: spec/04-access-control.md:166-168 (API), :177-199
- Evidence: The API offers only "GET .../admission-key/..." and "PUT <host>/admission-key/<groupID>/members". The host accepts a list only if "its `seq` and `ts` are both greater than those of the list it replaces", and only if it is a pure Addition or a pure Removal relative to the current list. "The committer of every Commit that adds a DID ... MUST send this addition and receive a `204` before it delivers the Welcome". "The committer of every Commit that removes the last leaf of a DID MUST send this removal" (no timing given).
- Problem: (1) A client cannot learn the host's current list or `seq`, and the descriptor carries no `seq`, so after any other member's PUT it cannot build a list that the host accepts except by guessing. (2) How `seq` is derived is unspecified. (3) PUTs from successive Commits can reach the host out of order. Example: an epoch N+1 removal and an epoch N+2 addition arrive in the order N+2, N+1. The N+2 list is neither a pure addition nor a pure removal relative to the host's epoch N list, so it gets `403`, and its committer cannot deliver the Welcome even though its Commit won. (4) Nothing says that DMMembers is sent only after the Commit wins. A committer whose Commit loses may already have changed the host list: a removed member keeps getting admission keys, or a live member is dropped. Mutual-removal races resolve in host-arrival order, not in the order MLS chose. (5) An Addition may add any DID to `complianceLoggers`, and the host never removes such an entry, so any member can give an arbitrary DID permanent, unremovable admission-key access at the host.
- Suggested fix: Define `seq` as the MLS epoch the change takes effect in. Require DMMembers to be sent only after the Commit has won (§5.1.1). Let the host accept any list with a greater `seq` that is signed by a DID on the current list and that keeps every current `complianceLoggers` entry, dropping the pure-addition/pure-removal classification. Add `GET <host>/admission-key/<groupID>/members`, returning the current signed list, for members. Require that a `complianceLoggers` entry added at the host is the `loggerDID`/`recorderDID` named by the signer's Space configuration, or accept that loggers are unverifiable at the host and say so.
- Needs decision: yes. (a) `seq` = MLS epoch, accept any later list that keeps the loggers, add GET (recommended). (b) Keep the classification, add GET, and require retry with the current list on `409`/`403`. This still fails with out-of-order delivery.

### CRY3-5: A removal at the host that has no matching MLS removal strands the remaining members with no recovery
- Severity: P2
- Location: spec/04-access-control.md:197, :201, spec/04-access-control.md:270
- Evidence: Removal is accepted when "signed by a DID on the current list. The signer may remove itself, another member, or both". "The host rotates the key when it accepts one." §4.6.4: "Members MUST treat a Commit that changes `agora_admission_host` while the host's user is still a member as a policy violation".
- Problem: The host cannot see MLS state. Any member, or a member being removed in MLS who acts before the remover's PUT lands, can PUT a list that removes every other member. The host rotates and serves the key only to that member. The others are still MLS members but cannot fetch the new key. Once the old key is deregistered they share no admitted token, so they cannot publish anything, including a handover Commit. If the attacker is the host's user, handover is forbidden anyway. They are not on the host list, so they cannot PUT a correction. With MLS-only removal the remaining members can re-add a victim. Here no remaining member can act. This follows from the binding decision that the host accepts removals from any listed DID. The missing part is a recovery path.
- Suggested fix: Allow any member whose admission host refuses it (`403`) while it is still an MLS member to commit a handover (§4.6.4) to its own identity host, and remove the policy-violation rule for that case. Also require clients to keep the last admitted token's key and to commit the handover before the old key is deregistered (the host's grace period). Alternative: allow any member to move the host at any time, which fits "no member has authority over the rest".
- Needs decision: yes. (a) Handover allowed when the current host refuses a member (recommended; narrow). (b) Any member may move the host at any time. (c) Accept the lockout and document it in §12.

### CRY3-6: `admissionHostDID` is not authenticated, so the host's-user check can be steered
- Severity: P2
- Location: spec/04-access-control.md:210 (descriptor paragraph), :264, :270
- Evidence: "`admissionHostDID` is an optional descriptor field; absent, it means `createdBy`." The descriptor "carries no object signature; the committer's MLS sender signature authenticates it". There is no rule on who may set or change `admissionHostDID`.
- Problem: Any member can send a `DMGroupDescriptor` naming itself, or anyone, as `admissionHostDID` without changing `agora_admission_host`. Members that keep that descriptor then believe the real host's user has gone, so a GroupContextExtensions Commit moving the host to the attacker's identity host passes the policy-violation check. Alternatively, a real departure of the host's user triggers no handover. Members joining by Welcome take the field from the first descriptor they receive, which any member can send.
- Suggested fix: Members MUST ignore a descriptor whose `admissionHostDID` differs from the previous value, unless it arrives in the epoch created by a Commit that changed `agora_admission_host` and its value is that Commit's sender. Better: derive the host's user from the GroupContext history (the sender of the last Commit that set the extension, or the group creator) and drop the descriptor field.
- Needs decision: no

### CRY3-7: Handover fails when the next member is served by the same identity host, and a creation can be squatted
- Severity: P2
- Location: spec/04-access-control.md:195, :266-269
- Evidence: "**Creation.** The first list the host accepts for an `mlsGroupID`, signed by a DID the host serves". Handover step 1 "sends the current member list to its own identity host as a creation `DMMembers`".
- Problem: In an enterprise, every employee's identity host is the same provisioning service. When the initiator leaves, the lowest-leaf member's host is the current host, which already holds a list for that `mlsGroupID`, so the "creation" is refused. The flow defines no way forward. `admissionHostDID` keeps naming a non-member, so every member's client keeps trying, and the 24 h fallback repeats forever. Separately, a former member who knows the group ID can pre-create a list at its own host (any host that serves one of the remaining members), which blocks a later legitimate handover to that host.
- Suggested fix: If the handover target is the current host, skip steps 1 and 2 and send only an updated descriptor naming the new host's user (authenticated per CRY3-6). Or define the host's user at host level: handover is needed only when no remaining non-logger member is served by the current host. For squatting, let a creation replace an existing list at that host when the existing list's signer is not on the submitted list and the submitter is on the existing list, or have the host accept a creation only together with a proof of membership (for example, a fetch of the old host's key).
- Needs decision: yes. (a) Host-level "host's user" (recommended; removes the case). (b) Same-host shortcut in §4.6.4.

### CRY3-8: "One file per key" is ambiguous for revisions and retries, and with index nonces any reuse breaks GCM
- Severity: P2
- Location: spec/14-jmap.md:130
- Evidence: "generated by the uploader for this file only. A client MUST NOT use a `contentKey` for more than one file. Because a key seals exactly one file, the nonce can be the chunk index."
- Problem: The earlier construction used random nonces, so reusing a key was only a hygiene issue. Now chunk i is always sealed under nonce i. If a client seals two different plaintexts under one key, AES-GCM leaks the XOR of those chunks and the GHASH key, which allows forgery of chunks under that key. "File" is not defined for a `FileNode` (sequenced, editable) whose content is replaced, or for a client that resumes an interrupted upload after the local file has changed. Both are natural places to reuse the object's key.
- Suggested fix: State the invariant directly: "A `contentKey` seals exactly one plaintext. A client MUST NOT seal two different chunk plaintexts under the same (`contentKey`, i). Any change of content, including a new revision of the same `FileNode`, MUST use a fresh `contentKey`. A client that re-encrypts after an interruption MUST either use a fresh key or encrypt from a snapshot it has verified to be unchanged."
- Needs decision: no

### CRY3-9: `prev` = null on a new commit stream when the replaced stream is empty breaks the fork-detection chain
- Severity: P3
- Location: spec/14-jmap.md:170
- Evidence: "carries in `prev` the hash of the last stamp of the closed commit stream, or `null` if that stream has no stamps"; "A member MUST treat a stamp on a closed commit stream at a position after the one that position 0 of the new stream references as a fork".
- Problem: With two rotations close together (T1 to T2 to T3) and no Commit on T2, T3's position 0 has `prev` = null. T1's closing position is then referenced by nothing, so a stamp the sequencer adds to T1 after closing cannot be detected as a fork.
- Suggested fix: Carry in `prev` the hash of the last stamp of the most recent non-empty closed commit stream of the channel. Use `null` only when the channel has no earlier commit stamp.
- Needs decision: no

### CRY3-10: Broadcast poster checks against "current"/"newest" state make past posts undisplayable
- Severity: P3
- Location: spec/05-messaging.md:209, :211
- Evidence: "A member checks that `senderDID` holds a space role listed in `broadcasterRoles` under current Space state." "A non-member subscriber MUST NOT display a post whose `senderDID` is not listed for the channel in the newest `broadcasters` list it holds."
- Problem: Once a broadcaster leaves the role, every earlier valid post by it fails the check for members and non-members alike, so the channel's history loses those posts. A removed poster cannot post in later key epochs anyway, because rotation takes away the admission and content keys, so checking against current state adds no security.
- Suggested fix: Check `senderDID` against the state, or `broadcasters` list, in force for the post's `keyEpoch`. For a non-member, that is any signed view issued while that `keyEpoch` was current.
- Needs decision: no

### CRY3-11: Peer blob replication carries no uploader authorization
- Severity: P3
- Location: spec/10-relays.md:179-185, :649-651
- Evidence: Eager replication `POST /v1/agora/peer/blob?token=...` is accepted when the token is registered and the blob "verifies it against its CID, charges it to that agreement's storage quota".
- Problem: Direct uploads need an `Agora-Storage-Auth` admission signature (§10.4.4). Replicated blobs need only relay-to-relay authentication. A misbehaving Relay that carries a token can push arbitrary blobs to every other Relay carrying it and use up the CO's storage quota there. No member key is needed.
- Suggested fix: Forward the original `Agora-Storage-Auth` value with each eagerly replicated blob. The receiving Relay verifies `admissionSig`, `sha256` and `expires`, and checks `nonce` against its own replay set. Pull-through and `seed` stay as they are, since a member request or an admission-signed `seed` triggers them.
- Needs decision: no
